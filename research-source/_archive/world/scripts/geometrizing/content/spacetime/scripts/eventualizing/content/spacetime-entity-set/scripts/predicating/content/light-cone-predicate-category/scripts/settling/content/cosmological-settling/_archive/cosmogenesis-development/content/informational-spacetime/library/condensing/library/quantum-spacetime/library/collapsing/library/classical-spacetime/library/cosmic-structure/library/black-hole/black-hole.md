## Bekenstein–Hawking entropy is the sheaf's boundary data — which is why it is *area*

A black hole is the highest-entropy object of its size, with entropy

$$ S_{BH} = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4 G \hbar} = \frac{k_B A}{4 l_P^2}, $$

proportional to **horizon area, not interior volume**.
The descent says why directly: the interior is a *thermal sheaf* ($H^1=0$), and a sheaf is determined by its global section, whose gluing data lives on the **boundary**.
Once thermalizing has completed, the interior carries no further distinguishable content — it is one glued value — so all the entropy that can still be told apart is the boundary's.
Area-not-volume is the holographic principle, and here it is not an extra postulate: it is the statement that *a thermal region's information is its boundary's gluing data*.
The horizon is exactly that boundary.

## Hawking radiation and the information paradox

Hawking re-evaporation is the **slow re-opening of the local $H^1$**: the thermal pocket leaking back toward the not-yet-thermal field, one quantum at a time, each carrying away exactly the charge that keeps the boundary balance.
The apparent information *loss* is the descent's own [forgetting](../../../../../../../../../../../forgetting/forgetting.md) (η, non-injective): an outside observer reads only the partial distribution $p_{\text{ext}}$ and cannot invert it.
But forgetting is non-injective *locally*, not globally — the glued global section retains what any single local reading drops.
So in the full joint manifold the divergence is conserved and nothing is destroyed; the paradox is the local lossiness of η mistaken for a global one.
