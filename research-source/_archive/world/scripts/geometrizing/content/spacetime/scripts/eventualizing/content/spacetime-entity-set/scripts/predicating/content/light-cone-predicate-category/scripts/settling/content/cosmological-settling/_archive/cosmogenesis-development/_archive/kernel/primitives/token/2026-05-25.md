Token is one of the two non-assembling primitives of the system.
The other is claim.
An utterance is the pair of one token and one claim.

## Non-assembling means inductive base

A token is not built from anything.
The schema declares it as a string only because strings are the system's chosen surface form for primitives — the string is the on-disk presentation, not the categorical content.
Categorically, a token is the atomic identity-carrying side of an utterance with no further decomposition.
Nothing assembles a token out of smaller pieces; tokens are where the recursion of articulating bottoms out.

This makes tokens the inductive base for the proof-engine character of the active tree.
The recursive type articulating has no finite inhabitant in the abstract — but a concrete inhabitant exists once an innermost utterance's token and claim are reached.
The string-typed schema is what makes the leaves finitely representable on disk.

## Token versus claim

Token and claim are dual.
Both are non-assembling primitives.
Both are string-typed on disk.
They occupy different positions in an utterance — token first, claim second.
Whatever distinguishes them categorically is what the token-before-claim working theory of the aperiodic-predication-cell partition is named for: under that theory, articulating naturally proceeds by first articulating the token side and only then the claim side.
Whether token-before-claim is the right reading is open work; the symmetric and reversed readings are alternatives.

## Token is not the string it is presented as

A token's identity does not reduce to the string contents.
Two tokens with the same string body in different utterances may carry different categorical roles; what makes them the same token is having the same role in the articulating recursion, not having matching string bodies.
The string is presentation, not identity.
Future work that needs token-identity beyond string-equality will live downstream of this primitive, not at it.

## Why two primitives, not one

A single non-assembling primitive would not give utterance the pair structure utterance needs to be articulating's domain.
Articulating articulates an utterance by articulating either its token side, its claim side, both, or neither — the four-cell partition of articulating's codomain requires the two-sidedness of utterance.
Collapsing to one primitive would collapse the four cells to two (articulated or not) and lose the cardinality the system relies on downstream.

A third primitive would oversaturate: three independent binary statuses give 2³ = 8 cells, and there is no derivation of an 8-cell partition in the system.
The cardinality two is forced by the four-cell partition the system needs.
