Claim is one of the two non-assembling primitives of the system.
The other is token.
An utterance is the pair of one token and one claim.

## Non-assembling means inductive base

A claim is not built from anything.
The schema declares it as a string because the on-disk presentation needs to be finite, but the categorical content of a claim has no further decomposition.
Nothing assembles a claim out of smaller pieces; claims are where the recursion of articulating bottoms out on the claim side.

Together with token, claim provides the inductive base that lets the recursive type articulating have concrete finite inhabitants.
Without these two primitives the recursion would be uninhabited.

## Claim versus token

Token and claim are dual non-assembling primitives.
They occupy different positions in an utterance — claim second, token first.
Under the token-before-claim working theory, the natural progression of articulating proceeds by articulating the token side first and the claim side second; the stable-aperiodic-predication-cell at (no token, yes claim) is OFF that natural path because the claim has been articulated without its expected prior token.
Whether token-before-claim is the right reading is open work.

## Claim is not the string it is presented as

A claim's identity does not reduce to the string contents.
The string is presentation; categorical identity is the role the claim plays in the articulating recursion.
Future work that needs claim-identity beyond string-equality lives downstream of this primitive.

## Position of claim in an utterance

In the schema for utterance, the claim is the second element of the pair.
The ordering is load-bearing for the token-before-claim partition reading: which primitive is "first" determines which aperiodic-predication-cells are on the natural progression path and which are off it.
