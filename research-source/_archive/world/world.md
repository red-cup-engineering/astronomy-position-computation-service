In multiverse cosmology, *world* is a generic term for a single universe-like entity within a [multiverse](multiverse.md). It is intentionally broader than *universe*: a world may be

- a causally disconnected post-inflationary bubble (eternal inflation);
- a branch of a quantum-mechanical wavefunction (Everettian many-worlds);
- a separate vacuum in the string-theory landscape;
- a region with a different effective fundamental constant (varying-constant proposals);
- a distinct mathematical structure (Tegmark Level IV).

The word is chosen because *universe* in cosmology often means "everything causally connected to us" — by definition unique. *World* avoids that built-in singularity and lets the cosmologist talk about populations.

## Minimal structural requirements

For something to count as a world in the multiverse-cosmology sense, it should at minimum support:

1. **Internal connectedness.** Some notion of "things in this world can interact with each other," even if not directly.
2. **Internal time-like structure.** Some dynamical or change-tracking endomorphism is defined within it.
3. **Non-degenerate internal structure.** A world is not just a point; it has internal complexity that distinguishes one of its states from another.

These three are the empirical / informal version of the conditions formalized as `(W1)`, `(W2)`, `(W3)` in [world-as-nucleus](../../predicated-multiverse/objects/world-as-nucleus.md), where the same intuition is cast as conditions on an object in a relational topos.

## Worlds vs. observable universe

Our [observable universe](../../observation/_index.md) is at most one world (or a tiny region inside one). What we call "the universe" in single-universe cosmology is, in a multiverse picture, a slice or sub-region of a world. The parent term [`universe`](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) is left to single-universe contexts; *world* is reserved for the multiverse setting.

## Boundary with worlds in other senses

This is **not** the [logician's notion of "possible world"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/possible-worlds/), nor the [physicist's notion of "world line"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_line). It is a multiverse-cosmology term. The categorical formulation in [world-as-nucleus](../../predicated-multiverse/objects/world-as-nucleus.md) is closer to the cosmologist's worlds; the structural resemblance to other worlds-vocabulary is open and a matter for future work.
